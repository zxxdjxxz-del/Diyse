## 1. Scope and Canon Status

This sheet documents the technical seven-zone gatehouse route implemented for v0.09B.

The map is a prototype traversal demonstration. The old Yahtrean gatehouse identity, final architecture, exact historical placement, visual materials, story role, rewards, enemies, and dialogue remain provisional unless separately approved in the Active Master Canon.

The following are implementation-authoritative for the prototype:

- seven connected compositions;
- Cyanis as the sole normally visible field character;
- layered elevation-aware routes;
- Crest mechanism controlling bridge connectivity;
- protected upper crossing;
- Ruin-pulse timing corridor;
- summit payoff;
- two-way return shortcut.

---

## 2. Location Identity

- **Culture:** Yahtrean defensive architecture with an older Crest-operated infrastructure layer.
- **Biome:** exposed highland or fortified approach; final biome remains provisional.
- **Function:** gate control, defensive observation, regulated passage, and mechanical route control.
- **History:** damaged structure whose original bridge system no longer remains continuously connected.
- **Narrative emotion:** approach, discovery, spatial understanding, activation, tension, and elevated payoff.
- **Signature landmark:** upper gate and bridge visible before they become reachable.
- **Traversal identity:** vertical climb through layered routes, mechanism-created connectivity, timing corridor, and return shortcut.

---

## 3. Three Visible Time Layers

### Original Function

A functioning Yahtrean gatehouse controlled movement through a defended route using Crest machinery, upper bridges, observation platforms, and a summit gate.

### Transformation

Damage or abandonment disconnected the bridge mechanism, degraded route safety, and allowed Ruin energy to produce a repeating corridor hazard.

### Current Adaptation

Cyanis restores temporary traversal by operating the surviving Crest mechanism, using protected routes and safe pockets, then opening a return shortcut at the summit.

Final visual evidence for these layers remains a later art pass. v0.09B proves the navigation and state logic.

---

## 4. VII–X Source Trace

### FFVII

- Temple-style vertical route layering;
- upper and lower paths overlapping visually but remaining separate;
- environmental object becoming a traversable connection;
- timing hazard based on safe pockets;
- foreground occlusion and route indicators;
- loop completed by a shortcut.

### FFVIII

- gatehouse treated as a functioning defensive and mechanical facility;
- coherent local vocabulary centered on bridge control and route security;
- operation of one system changes practical circulation.

### FFIX

- compact connected compositions rather than one oversized empty environment;
- layered architecture and foreground framing;
- damaged infrastructure changing topology.

### FFX

- clear directed route with side-safe pockets;
- changing spatial scale and camera composition;
- contextual mechanism interaction;
- visible historical layers of original use, damage, and present adaptation.

## 5. Spatial Grammar

Primary grammars:

- vertical climb;
- gated network;
- loop with shortcut;
- timing-hazard corridor;
- destination foreshadowing.

Route sequence:

`Approach → Stair Court → Lower Arch Passage → Crest Mechanism → Upper Bridge → Ruin-Pulse Corridor → Summit Gate`

Return sequence after summit activation:

`Summit Gate ↔ Return Shortcut ↔ Lower Route`

The bridge connection remains inactive until the Crest mechanism resolves.

## 6. Seven Compositions

| Zone | Purpose | Primary lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Distant Approach | Establish destination and broad movement | landmark foreshadowing and readable critical path |
| Layered Stair Court | Introduce vertical relationship | stairs, scaling, camera-relative diagonal movement |
| Lower Arch Passage | Cross beneath future upper route | foreground masking and upper/lower separation |
| Crest Mechanism Chamber | Change route state | contextual alignment and dynamic connectivity |
| Protected Upper Bridge | Traverse the earlier overhead route | safe narrow-looking lane and elevation payoff |
| Ruin-Pulse Corridor | Test timing | readable cycle, safe pockets, checkpoint reset |
| Summit Gate | Deliver destination payoff | state completion and return shortcut |

## 7. Movement and Collision

v0.09B preserves the v0.09A1 movement baseline:

- camera-relative analog touch movement;
- very short acceleration and deceleration;
- forgiving wall sliding;
- smooth elevation continuity;
- camera-basis blending;
- protected edges;
- broad route connections;
- no ordinary accidental falls.

The end-to-end automated route test must reach the summit only after mechanism activation.

---

## 8. Camera and Occlusion

Seven authored camera compositions correspond to the seven route zones.

Requirements:

- held input continues naturally across boundaries;
- upper and lower paths remain readable;
- Cyanis retains minimum readable scale;
- foreground arches fade only when necessary;
- occlusion state restores correctly;
- transition spawns do not immediately retrigger the prior camera.

---

## 9. Mechanism Vocabulary

The prototype’s local vocabulary is intentionally limited:

1. Crest interaction point;
2. inactive route connection;
3. bridge activation;
4. safe-pocket hazard navigation;
5. summit shortcut activation.

The mechanism chamber teaches that operating the surviving Crest device creates a new walkmesh connection. The summit teaches that completion can permanently improve return circulation.

---

## 10. Ruin-Pulse Hazard

The corridor includes:

- a repeating visible pulse cycle;
- a warning phase;
- an active hazard lane;
- three generous safe pockets;
- checkpoint advancement;
- knockback or reset to the last safe position;
- no requirement for precision balance or platforming.

Device acceptance must confirm that the cycle is understandable without trial-and-error frustration.

---

## 11. Automated Verification

The v0.09B workflow verifies:
